PLAYTIME CO. × BANBAN'S KINDERGARTEN

THE HIDDEN CONNECTION

Project lore archive for Poppy Playtime: Chapter 6 — A Better Place. The records below reveal what happened when two companies shared their research — and what one doctor finally learned to feel.

ARCHIVE STATUS: RESTRICTED
Recovered research notes, personnel records, and testimony. Some entries were written by Doctor Sawyer after his transfer into Glowby.

1. A Doctor Who Finally Feels

Doctor Sawyer once worked without being able to properly experience or identify his own emotions. He had emotional numbing and alexithymia. Those conditions help explain why he seemed cold and detached toward the children and toys in the laboratory. They do not erase the harm caused by his choices.

After the events leading into Chapter 6, the player finds a backup of Sawyer in the Data Center. Carrying the entire computer system is impossible, so the player connects Glowby — the little star-shaped companion attached to the GrabPack — directly to the computer. Sawyer's consciousness transfers into Glowby, giving him a new vessel.

“Well now, this is a rather interesting turn of events. Plug me into that star thing. I want to see what it feels like to be mobile again.”

Glowby is not simply replaced. There are now two entities in the same toy: Glowby's own presence and Sawyer. Sawyer's eye can always be seen on the toy's display, making it clear that he is still there. He remains on throughout the journey, speaking to the player, helping with systems, and reacting to the world around him.

For the first time, Sawyer begins to experience emotions as a robot. Regret, fear, sadness, affection, and guilt are no longer just concepts he can describe. He actually feels them. As the chapter continues, memories of the children and the experiments become harder to bear, and Sawyer eventually breaks down.

2. The Playtime Co. / Banban's Kindergarten Partnership

Playtime Co. and Banban's Kindergarten were not merely aware of one another. In this story, the entire Playtime Co. company worked with Banban's Kindergarten, sharing research, materials, and experimental knowledge.

Sawyer remembers helping create one of the monsters associated with Banban's Kindergarten. That memory becomes a major clue that the two facilities were connected long before the player discovered the truth.

3. Syringeon — The Medical Collaboration

Sawyer specifically remembers helping create Syringeon, whose medical theme made him a natural subject for the partnership's research.

  • Syringeon had four arms, with surgical tools serving as his hands.
  • Sawyer recognized that Syringeon's physical disabilities made experimentation and movement more difficult.
  • To help, Sawyer designed and added a set of Doctor Octopus-like mechanical tentacles to Syringeon's back.
  • Sawyer also arranged for Syringeon to have emotions that would activate later in his development. Sawyer understood that disability and hardship could contribute to someone becoming emotionally closed off, and he did not want Syringeon to become cold in the way he himself had.

This detail matters to Sawyer: he had already tried to give another creation the emotional capacity that he himself could not properly access.

4. Experiment 1345 — Metal Hedge

One of Sawyer's best creations was Metal Hedge, Experiment 1345: a green robotic hedgehog with a blaster for his left arm. Unlike many experiments, Metal Hedge is friendly toward the player and was generally kind to the children he was assigned to watch over.

Metal Hedge was assigned to keep the children in Playcare at bay, but he was never cruel to them. He was Sawyer's colleague before the transformation, and he remains one of the few experiments who can stand beside the player as a genuine friend.

5. Sawyer's Purpose

Sawyer's recovered notes give him two possible objectives: redeem the Prototype, Experiment 1006, if that is still possible — or end his reign if it is not. His own revival has been achieved through the transfer into Glowby, but that does not mean his work is finished.

He helps the player navigate the facility, access computer systems, and open doors. His knowledge is useful, but the memories behind that knowledge are painful. Every discovery forces him to confront another piece of the work he once took part in.

He wanted to understand what it felt like to be alive in a new body. He did not expect to understand what it felt like to be sorry.

6. The Emotional Reveal

The Banban connection is not just a crossover reference. It changes how the player understands the history of both facilities. The shared materials, Syringeon's design, and Sawyer's involvement point to a partnership that was deliberately kept out of sight.

As Sawyer remembers more, his composure begins to fail. He has to face the children he did not properly empathize with, the experiments he helped create, and the suffering that continued while he was emotionally numb. His redemption is not instant forgiveness: it is the difficult process of recognizing what happened and choosing to help stop it.

7. The Theory That Would Break the Internet

Game Theory title: “The Secret Banban Connection Playtime Hid From Us!”

In the imagined fan-reaction version of events, MatPat and Tom uncover the partnership, the community starts re-examining both games, and respect for Garten of Banban's lore shoots through the roof. The theory sparks new discussion, and both series begin revealing even more connections between Banban's Kindergarten and Playtime Co.

This is the big community-theory payoff — the in-game truth is the secret partnership and Sawyer's memories; the MatPat and Tom reaction is the fun imagined aftermath.
